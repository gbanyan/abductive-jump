Dear Editors,

Please consider our Article, “Measuring representation change in language-model discovery,” for publication in *Nature Machine Intelligence*.

The paper addresses a measurement gap in current evaluations of language-model hypothesis generation. Existing studies can establish that a candidate is useful, surprising or high performing, but generally cannot determine whether it escaped the representational language supplied by the task. We introduce a preregistered prospective assay that freezes an incumbent grammar and its best admissible predictor, certifies structural non-membership, and requires the candidate to outperform that oracle on an intervention committed before its outcome is observed.

The contribution is both methodological and mechanistic. Across controlled synthetic worlds, representation-changing proposal mechanisms separate sharply from matched fixed-space alternatives. A proposal–reasoning factorial localizes the limiting component under a frozen model interface. A second preregistered phase removes high-level family operators and shows that local generic rewrites can be composed into validated candidates, including for a held-out arity-three structural family. Exact simulators, false-positive controls and complete deterministic replay make the claim auditable.

We believe the work will interest NMI readers concerned with language-model reasoning, scientific agents, benchmark validity and computational creativity. The paper does not claim autonomous scientific discovery or human-like abduction. Its scope is deliberately narrower: a reproducible way to test representation-level escape and evidence that proposal and reasoning can be experimentally separated in the registered systems.
