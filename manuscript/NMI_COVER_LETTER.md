Dear Editors,

Please consider our Article, “A prospective assay for hypothesis-space expansion in AI systems,” for publication in *Nature Machine Intelligence*.

The paper addresses a measurement and attribution gap in evaluations of AI-assisted scientific discovery. We freeze an incumbent hypothesis language and its best admissible predictor, certify structural non-membership, and require a candidate to outperform that predictor on an intervention committed before its outcome is observed and on independent falsification cases.

Across controlled synthetic worlds, typed proposals separate sharply from fixed-space alternatives. Generic local rewrites compose into validated candidates in 400/400 known-family and 100/100 held-out-family worlds. Crucially, a post-hoc component audit found that the successful C3 path did not causally require language-model output: a deterministic no-inference replay reproduced all 2,400 candidate verdicts, 500/500 jump-world successes and 0/300 control false jumps. We report this result directly and locate the contribution in the prospective assay and typed search scaffold.

The work complements recent open-space discovery systems by combining canonical non-membership, outcome-before-commitment evaluation, an exact incumbent oracle, candidate-level gate attrition, specificity controls and deterministic replay. It also demonstrates how code-path auditing can prevent a surrounding scaffold from being misattributed to a language model.

The study does not claim autonomous scientific discovery or broad model competence. C3 is saturated, C_self is a serialization-interface result, and the single held-out family is conceptually adjacent. We propose multi-model, repaired-interface and independently authored noisy-family extensions as future experiments rather than presenting them as completed evidence.
