Please consider our Article, “A prospective assay for hypothesis-space expansion in AI systems,” for publication in *Nature Machine Intelligence*.

The paper addresses a measurement and attribution gap in evaluations of AI-assisted scientific discovery. We freeze an incumbent hypothesis language and its best admissible predictor, certify structural non-membership, and require a candidate to outperform that predictor on an intervention committed before its outcome is observed and on independent falsification cases.

Across controlled synthetic worlds, typed proposals separate sharply from fixed-space alternatives. Generic local rewrites compose into validated candidates in 400/400 known-family and 100/100 held-out-family worlds. Crucially, a post-hoc component audit found that C3 did not causally require language-model output: deterministic replay reproduced all 2,400 candidate verdicts, 500/500 jump-world successes and 0/300 control false jumps. This negative attribution result is a central contribution. It demonstrates how a system-level scientific-discovery benchmark can misattribute successful content to a model when deterministic scaffolding supplies the decisive representation, fit and intervention.

We also report a deliberately minimal sensitivity extension rather than a costly factorial replication. On the same outcome-blind 96-world panel, autonomous proposal remained 0/96 after increasing Phi-4 precision, increasing only its completion cap, substituting a stronger DeepSeek checkpoint under the matched interface, using its native reasoning mode or allowing one validator-only repair. Attrition remained predominantly pre-executable. A balanced supplied-representation control succeeded in 3/40 worlds, establishing limited ability to use a correct representation while revealing that the native reasoning/output interface was itself strongly budget-limited. These results rule out several simple explanations without presenting the subset as a replacement confirmation.

The work complements recent open-space discovery systems by combining a canonical out-of-space test, outcome-before-commitment evaluation, an exact incumbent oracle, candidate-level gate attrition, specificity controls and deterministic replay. Its contribution is a measurement and causal-attribution framework rather than a claim of autonomous model discovery.

The study does not claim autonomous scientific discovery, broad model competence or a model-independent conceptual incapacity. C3 is saturated, all autonomous sensitivity conditions remained dominated by the proposal interface, and the single held-out family is conceptually adjacent. Independently authored noisy families and a grammar-valid interface with separately reserved reasoning and answer budgets remain future experiments.
