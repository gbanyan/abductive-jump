Dear Editors,

Please consider our Article, “A prospective assay for hypothesis-space expansion in AI systems,” for publication in *Nature Machine Intelligence*.

The paper addresses a measurement and attribution gap in evaluations of AI-assisted scientific discovery. We freeze an incumbent hypothesis language and its best admissible predictor, certify structural non-membership, and require a candidate to outperform that predictor on an intervention committed before its outcome is observed and on independent falsification cases.

Across controlled synthetic worlds, typed proposals separate sharply from fixed-space alternatives. A deterministic four-step rewrite search paired with a fixed motif-to-basis realizer produced validated candidates in 400/400 known-family and 100/100 held-out-family worlds. Crucially, a post-hoc component audit reproduced every candidate verdict without model output. This negative attribution result is a central contribution: it demonstrates how a system-level scientific-discovery benchmark can misattribute successful content when deterministic scaffolding supplies the representation search, realization basis, fit, ranking and intervention.

We also report a deliberately minimal sensitivity extension rather than a costly factorial replication. On the same fixed 96-world panel, self-composition remained 0/96 after Phi-4 precision and budget changes, substitution of a second locally served DeepSeek checkpoint, native reasoning or one validator-only repair. Contract audit identified a prompt-executor mismatch and a reasoning-to-answer failure. We therefore froze one grammar-constrained DeepSeek condition with separate deliberation and answer budgets, explicit argument syntax and no semantic or outcome feedback. It made 3,939/4,608 plans executable and yielded 15/96 validated worlds, concentrated in two families. Random four-step composition yielded 16/96 on the same panel, and every validated model candidate triggered one scaffold-supplied realizer motif. The sensitivity therefore exposes model-generated graph topology hidden by the legacy interfaces without showing aggregate superiority over random search or model-authored scientific laws.

A separately frozen, inference-free counterfactual audit then isolated the realizer itself. Disabling motif realization reduced C3 from 400/400 known-family and 100/100 held-out worlds to zero, reduced random composition from 52/400 and 13/100 to zero, and reduced grammar-constrained DeepSeek from 15/96 to zero. Most candidate slots nevertheless retained structural non-membership and observational adequacy before failing prospective discrimination. Retaining motif algebra while removing role- and action-aware field binding preserved 447/500 C3 and 8/96 model-proposed successes. These results identify the fixed semantic basis library as a necessary scaffold component while distinguishing it from the more limited contribution of field binding.

The work complements recent open-space discovery systems by combining a canonical out-of-space test, outcome-before-commitment evaluation, an exact incumbent oracle, candidate-level gate attrition, specificity controls and deterministic replay. Its contribution is a measurement and causal-attribution framework rather than a claim of autonomous model discovery.

The study does not claim autonomous scientific discovery, broad model competence or a model-independent conceptual incapacity. The deterministic search is saturated, grammar-constrained success occurred in only two of eight families, and the single held-out family is conceptually adjacent and search-side only. Independently authored noisy families, deeper compositions and broader model comparisons remain future experiments.
